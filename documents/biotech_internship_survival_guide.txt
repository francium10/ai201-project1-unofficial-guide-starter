Title: The Unofficial Biotech/Pharma Internship Survival Guide (Community Compiled)

This guide is compiled from hundreds of r/biotech posts over 3 years. It covers what no recruiter will tell you.

COMPENSATION BENCHMARKS (2024):
- Big Pharma regulatory (Merck, BMS, J&J, Pfizer): $25-32/hr
- Big Pharma data science/bioinformatics: $38-48/hr
- Big Pharma lab/research scientist: $22-28/hr
- Pharma-tech SaaS (Veeva, Benchling, Medidata): $45-55/hr
- TechBio/AI drug discovery (Recursion, Relay, Insitro): $48-58/hr
- Clinical-stage biotech startup: $20-30/hr (often with equity discussion for PPO roles)

Housing stipends: About 60% of Big Pharma companies offer them ($2,000-3,500/summer). Pure tech companies (Veeva, Benchling) rarely do. Factor this in when comparing offers.

WHAT ACTUALLY MATTERS IN INTERVIEWS:
1. Know the FDA regulatory pathway relevant to the role:
   - Drug roles: IND → Phase 1/2/3 → NDA/BLA
   - Device roles: 510(k) for Class II, PMA for Class III
   - Know what an IND is (Investigational New Drug application)
2. Know ICH guidelines at a high level (Q1-Q13 cover different aspects of pharma development)
3. Be able to explain your research clearly to a non-expert — this separates candidates at every level
4. Have genuine answers to "why this company specifically" — generic answers kill applications

RETURN OFFER RATES (community estimates, vary by year):
- Genentech: ~40-50% for strong performers
- Merck: ~30-40%
- BMS: ~35-45%
- Pfizer: ~20-30% (post-2023 layoffs reduced this)
- Veeva: ~50-60%
- Benchling: ~40-50%
- Recursion: ~30-40%
- J&J MedTech: ~25-30%
- Tempus AI: ~40-50%

BIGGEST MISTAKES INTERNS MAKE:
1. Not networking. In pharma, who you know matters enormously for full-time roles. Schedule coffee chats proactively.
2. Waiting to be given work. Especially at startups, you need to pull work toward you.
3. Not understanding their project's business context. Know how your work fits the product/pipeline.
4. Treating compliance training as a box to check. GxP, HIPAA, SOPs — these exist for real reasons. Taking them seriously marks you as professional.
5. Not asking for feedback at the midpoint. By the time your end review happens, it's too late to correct course.

TIMELINE TIPS:
- Big Pharma (Merck, Pfizer, BMS, J&J) open applications in August-October for the following summer
- Pharma-tech (Veeva, Benchling) open September-December
- TechBio startups: rolling, often January-March
- Apply early — many programs fill before the official deadline

SKILLS THAT DIFFERENTIATE:
- Regulatory knowledge (IND/NDA/BLA/510k/PMA) for regulatory roles
- Python + bioinformatics tools (Biopython, GATK, NextFlow) for computational roles
- Veeva Vault familiarity (used everywhere in pharma for document management)
- GxP documentation practices
- Scientific writing and communication
