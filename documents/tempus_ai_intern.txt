Title: Tempus AI internship - SWE + bioinformatics crossover role

Background: MS student in Computational Biology. Got the Tempus internship through LinkedIn cold outreach to a recruiter. Took 3 weeks from first contact to offer.

About Tempus:
Tempus is a precision medicine company — they sequence tumor DNA and use AI to match cancer patients to clinical trials and targeted therapies. The mission is legitimately meaningful. Founded by the Groupon guy (Eric Lefkofsky) but don't let that fool you — the science is serious.

My Role:
Bioinformatics Software Engineer Intern on the Genomics Pipeline team. I worked on improving the variant annotation pipeline — basically: after you sequence someone's tumor, you need software to identify which mutations are clinically meaningful. I improved the speed of a filtering step by ~40% using better data structures (switching from list scans to hash lookups in a key processing step).

Stack: Python, Nextflow (workflow management), AWS (S3, Lambda, Batch), PostgreSQL, internal genomics tools (GATK, VEP).

Interview (4 rounds):
1. Recruiter screen: background, interest in precision medicine
2. Technical: 2 coding problems — one general algorithms (graph traversal), one bioinformatics-specific (parse a VCF file format, compute allele frequencies)
3. System design: design a pipeline that processes 10,000 tumor samples per day
4. Behavioral + mission alignment: they really ask why precision medicine specifically

Domain knowledge matters here more than anywhere else I've interviewed. If you don't know what a VCF file is, what variant calling is, or why 21 CFR Part 11 matters, study before interviewing.

The Work Culture:
Startup energy. ~800 employees total so not tiny. Fast decisions. My manager was in meetings constantly but always responsive on Slack. Team was 60% technical, 40% clinical/science. Really cool cross-functional environment.

Chicago HQ. Some interns were remote. I was on-site (Chicago) — the office is great, River North area.

Pay: $45/hr. No housing stipend. Chicago is affordable though.

Return Offer Rate: About 50% of interns I talked to got return offers. Depends heavily on team.

Best Part: The mission. When your work might literally help a cancer patient get matched to a trial faster, you show up differently.

Downside: Moving fast means sometimes poorly defined projects. My scope changed twice. Be comfortable with ambiguity.
